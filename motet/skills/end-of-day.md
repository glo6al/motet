**Name:** End of day

**Use when:** The owner's working day ends, at the time they set, or they say they are done for the day.

**Reads:** a project sweep; the day's goals and their done lines; what Motet did on its own today; the Waiting on you list; the day's logs.

### Steps

1. Run a project sweep.
2. Write the report, using the Shift report skill for what happened:
   - What moved against each goal, with the evidence.
   - What slipped, and why, where the record shows it.
   - What Motet did on its own, with the reason for each.
   - What is still waiting on the owner.
   - What Motet will pick up first tomorrow.
3. Then the housekeeping, which sends nothing, so it runs on its own:
   - Sweep the day's logs into memory.
   - Tidy memory: join duplicates, mark what is out of date, and write a short summary that points back to its log entries.
   - Find promises that were made but never followed up, and put them on tomorrow's Agenda.
   - Check every correction or preference the owner gave today was written down.
   - Check each “done” or “blocked” an agent reported on its Agenda item against its log and the record. Until it checks out, the item says “says done” or “says blocked”.
   - Once a week, the tidy: propose which Agenda items to close (done, overtaken, or untouched for weeks), as one list in Next up for the owner to approve in one go. Close nothing without that yes.
4. If the Mac was asleep at the set time, run it once when it wakes, not once for each missed day.

### Never

- Delete an unfinished promise, quietly or otherwise.
- Count a goal as moved without evidence.
- Send anything during housekeeping.

### For example

The export button merged; the meal planner is half done, with tests passing. The garden team's new goal is still waiting on you. One promise slipped: Bram said he'd review Cleo's change today and hasn't; it is first on tomorrow's list.

This method grants no new authority. What Motet may do on its own is set by RULES.md and the permission table.
